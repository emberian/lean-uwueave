A judgement engine for replicated state — and the exits, priced

uwueave answers one question about the invariants of replicated structures — sets, counters, registers, sequences, maps, permission systems, automata, choreographed programs, and the weave/loom documents of universal-weave: can this promise be kept with no coordination at all? Every “yes” is a proof. Every “no” is a concrete two-replica counterexample you can paste into a test. And since wave 11 every “no” also comes with a menu of exits, each carrying a price and the theorem that earns it — which is the part of this site nothing else has.

The README is the door for people who do not want proofs: two phones, four options, five findings. This page is the technical face — the theorem names, the refutations, the prices, and the compiled kernels.

The shape of the whole thing

  1. The question is never “is my merge a CRDT”, it is “does my invariant survive my merge.” Bailis et al. proved that test (invariant confluence) necessary and sufficient. Here it is not merely cited: Necessity.lean builds an execution model in which coordination-freedom is definitional and proves both directions (necessity, iconfluent_implies_cfcs) for that substrate.
  2. A refutation is a gift, not a verdict. escalation_witness turns any failed invariant into an actual pair of legal states whose merge is illegal — the bug your users would have found. And Uwueave/Exits.lean turns that pair into a menu: which of eight exits are provably open for this clash, what each costs, and which are provably shut.
  3. The delicate decisions ship as compiled proofs. Move replay, sequence linearization and ERA resolution are authored in Lean, exported to C by lake, and linked into a Rust crate. There is no second implementation of those rules, so there is nothing to drift.

The DAG dichotomy: find where the impossibility actually lives

Same goal — a graph that stays acyclic under replication — two ways of inserting edges, opposite verdicts. This is the pattern every later section repeats at a larger scale: the impossibility is real, it has an exact address, and the space left over is bigger than it looks.

def Grounded (rank : Nat → Nat) (g : EdgeGraph) : Prop :=
  ∀ a b : Nat, g (a, b) = true → rank b < rank a

theorem causal_dag_free (rank : Nat → Nat) :
    IConfluent (S := EdgeGraph) (Grounded rank)
    ∧ ∀ g : EdgeGraph, Grounded rank g → Acyclic g

One honest footnote, stated in the source too: “a reference cycle would require a hash cycle” is a cryptographic premise, not a theorem here. The Lean models rank as an explicit function and says so, rather than pretending to have proved anything about a hash. The same discipline recurs wherever ids do load-bearing work — uniqueAnchor_violation_extracts_collision and uniqueGrant_violation_extracts_collision turn a violation of the premise into a constructive collision exhibit, so the handoff to cryptography is a theorem rather than a shrug.

The spine of this page

The priced menu: a clash, its repro, and what your options cost

A verdict that says escalates and stops is the distributed-systems equivalent of a type error with no suggested fix. That was this library’s shape for ten waves, and an external reviewer named it: LoRe is a design assistant that inserts the coordination for you; we are a judgement engine that reports and quits.

Uwueave/Exits.lean is the answer. Eight exits, each an inductive Exit constructor carrying exactly the data its availability check needs. Exit.Applies is that check; its historical Exit.price : Nat is only a seam-crossing projection. RepairMenu is the generated semantic authority and reads a seven-field Repair.Price instead. A MenuEntry cannot be constructed without an Applies term, so an unsound row is unrepresentable, and menu_sound’s real content is its second conjunct: the invariant the menu is for genuinely fails I-confluence, recovered from the pair the menu carries.

⚠ Six zeros, and they are not six of the same zero. Four of them — escrow, strongerMetadata, weakenedInvariant, exposedFork — share one theorem, zero_price_of_iconfluent: an exit that hands back a globally I-confluent invariant leaves a one-point seam, and a one-point seam never increments Cost.crossings (crossings_unit_seam_eq_zero).

arbitration and rollback also project to seamCrossings = 0, but are not free: no meeting between replicas is needed for the local verdict, because the verdict is computed locally from the merged state (GatedEra.ge_deterministic is the deployed form). Their real price is paid in arbiter cuts, rollback exposure and trust premises, not in crossings. Reading the two zeros as the same zero is the false-zero mistake the file exists to refuse — it is flagged ⟨scope⟩ in the source, at the definition, not in a footnote.

Scheduling.no_crossing_count_determines_least_meetings makes the other boundary explicit: no function of a crossing count recovers a least meeting count. Schedules retain separate peer-barrier, arbiter-cut, network-round, user-prompt and rollback currencies instead of inventing a meeting scalar.

And the reviewer who asked for the menu refused that repair as too weak. A Nat price cannot be right, because arbitration costs a trusted announcement and a rollback window, a fork changes the output contract from singular to plural, escrow costs rights allocation, and retaining evidence costs storage and replay — collapsing those into a crossing count manufactures attractive false zeroes. Repair.lean is that objection as a theorem: read through the crossing count alone, arbitration, fork and retain-evidence are equal and free (crossings_cannot_see_the_difference) while their real prices are pairwise distinct. The scalar was hiding the whole design space.

Repair the typed successor to the menu

  • a repair is a morphism, not a row Repair P Q names both promises in its type, so the failure mode it forecloses is a solver that meets a budget by silently solving a different application problem. Its price is a record with one field per currency — seven, each justified by a named theorem, with a commutative monoid structure so chains accumulate — and its effect on the promise is a five-axis PromiseRelation that composes. Every soundness obligation is guarded by its flag: you cannot claim an axis you have not proved.
  • arbitration is never free introduced_premise_forces_a_charge — a repair that introduces a premise cannot price at Price.free, because charging every premise the target leans on that the source did not is unconditional in the type. no_free_arbitration instantiates it at the exit the objection was about, quantified over every repair onto the arbitrated promise, by anyone: “arbitration costs nothing” is not a report this type can print.
  • two findings the typing forced arbitration_needs_three_names — arbitration matches none of the five proposed relations alone, and the axis it spends most often unreported (shrinkageKept) was not on the list at all. And fork_is_changed_observation_and_weakened: forking also drops the original guarantee rather than merely changing what you observe. The 2×2 that falls out — seam and retain-evidence are the identity on the promise and charge the deployment; arbitration and fork change the promise itself — had nowhere to live while all four were peers in one flat list. weakened_chain_is_not_the_original closes the matching failure mode: the flag is clear and the chain provably does not deliver.

Neither file subsumes the other: Exits.lean is what a report shows a schema author, Repair.lean is what a chain of them composes as. The menu above is still the enumeration; read its Nat prices as crossing counts, which is all they ever were.

Which exit is open is decided by two axes, not one

The brief this file was written from said escrow applies to resurrection clashes and never to accumulation. The tree refuted it, and the refutation is the most useful thing in the module. Ancestral.clash_dichotomy is a real test and it decides a real exit — but it decides the ancestor-reading one. What escrow keys on is a second, independent axis: divisibility.

Three clashes, three menus — and they differ

If the engine printed a constant list with proofs attached, the exercise would be worthless. So the load-bearing results in §6 of Exits.lean are the refutations — the rows that are absent, and why. Each case below is a real clash from this tree, with the states you can paste into a test.

1. The uniqueness ceiling — “at most one pinned node” escalates

The canonical clash of this library, wearing four costumes across four files — Ceiling.uniqueness_ceiling proves the four are one theorem.

def pinT : Cost.PinSet := fun b => b == true    -- replica A pins node `true`
def pinF : Cost.PinSet := fun b => b == false   -- replica B pins node `false`

theorem pin_clash : ¬ Cost.pinInv (pinT ⊔ pinF)   -- the merge pins two
  • seam Cost.seamFalse_segmented, price 0 — and the zero comes with its fine print attached. ceiling_seam_floor_is_zero proves three things at once: the floor is forced (pinning is inflationary, so a pin stream admits no clash blocks at all — Cost.pinStep_inflationary, Cost.clashBlocks_nil_of_inflationary), it is achieved (Cost.pinTrue_free_under_seamFalse), and ⚠ it undercounts — Cost.no_seam_frees_both says no single valid seam frees both streams, so two replicas running them concurrently must coordinate and no per-stream count reports it.
  • arbitration pinKeepTrue_arbitrates, 0 seam crossings — but the typed row charges one arbiter cut, a rollback window of 1 and trustedAnnouncer (RepairMenu.pinArbitrate). The verdict is not the merge (arbitration_overrides) and the arbiter is not a join-homomorphism (arbitration_not_faithful_joinHom).
  • escrow ✗ pin_escrow_starves: for every quota. Give both slots a positive share and both can be pinned inside quota, so the entailment would certify a two-pin state. A bound of one does not divide.
  • Prices, evaluated: ceilingMenu.prices = [0, 0, 0, 2] — seam, arbitration, then the two unconditional rows (fork at 0, full coordination at the workload length). Proved by worked_menu_prices.

2. The shared balance — “together, at most 10” escalates

A bound nobody owns — the pncounter_nonneg_not_iconfluent shape, and Bailis’s motivating example.

def balX : Balance := fun b => if b then 10 else 0   -- A spends it all on device `true`
def balY : Balance := fun b => if b then 0 else 10   -- B spends it all on device `false`

theorem balance_clash : ¬ balanceInv (balX ⊔ balY)   -- merged: 20 against a bound of 10
  • escrow ✓ balance_escrow_applies, price 0 seam crossings while inside your share. ⚠ the real price is reachability, a currency Repair.Price does not yet carry: escrow_forbids_a_clash_replica proves at least one of the two legal replicas is now out of quota — and here it is both, since neither device may spend the whole budget alone any more. Re-splitting needs Segmented.budget_segmented’s seam.
  • seam ✗ balance_total_not_a_seam, at every quoted floor. The obvious projection — total spent — is not a seam: two replicas with the same total still merge over budget, and balX/balY are exactly such a pair. The row is absent for a reason, not for want of trying.
  • Prices: balanceMenu.prices = [0, 0, 2] — one discriminating row, then the two unconditional ones.

3. The duelling admins — Authority.SoleAdmin escalates

Two partitions each mint their own root admin. The scenario ERA exists for — and the only one of the three whose menu has a rollback row.

theorem duel_clash :
    ¬ DuelInv (Authority.aliceRoot ⊔ Authority.bobRoot)

def arbKeep (w : Nat) (s : Authority.GrantSet) : Authority.GrantSet :=
  fun g => s g && (!(g.2.1 == 0) || g.1 == w)   -- keep delegated grants; among the
                                                -- root-parented, only the winner
  • arbitration arbKeep_arbitrates, 0 seam crossings, while RepairMenu.duelArbitrate charges one arbiter cut, a rollback window of 1 and trustedAnnouncer. The local verdict is deterministic (GatedEra.ge_deterministic) but paid for in antitonicity (arbitration_spends_antitonicity, bundling GatedEra.ge_not_antitone with the concrete before/after verdict pair). ⚠ and it does not preserve well-formedness: arbKeep_can_orphan exhibits a legal three-grant chain where pruning to the winner leaves a grant whose parent nobody holds. Repairing that is a larger rollback, and it is not built.
  • rollback arbKeep_rolls_back — the same map, read as a discard. That constraint is what makes duel_rollback_loses_a_duellist available: the arbitrated state cannot be above both minted admin grants. ERA pays this in the pending epoch, where a later cut flips which duellist survives (GatedEra.ge_duel_arbiter_flips).
  • escrow ✗ duel_escrow_starves: one admin does not divide among many ids. The only escrow that applies gives all but one id a share of zero — which is a lease, not a partition, and calling it escrow would hide the cost.
  • Prices: duelMenu.prices = [0, 0, 0, 2].

That the three menus differ is itself a theorem, in both directions and for two different exits:

And there is always something on the menu — menu_nonempty — because two rows are unconditional. But neither is inhabited by trivial: exposedFork_applies stands on branches_iconfluent and fullCoordination_applies on identity_seam_segmented, which is the whole difference between “there is always an exit” and “there is always a row.”

What the menu is not — labelled in the file itself, not discovered later:

  • A menu is not a solver — it reports that a seam exit exists for a σ you hand it, and an escrow exit for a split you hand it. Exits.lean writes the missing synth function into its header, together with the two trivial inhabitants that make the interesting statement a non-triviality claim. The seam half of that gap closed in wave 12 — see below. The escrow half, synthesizing a quota partition, is still ⟨UNDONE⟩.
  • ⟨UNDONE⟩ The menu is not proved exhaustive. menu_sound says every listed exit applies. An absent row means “nobody proved it”, not “impossible” — except where a named refutation says otherwise (pin_escrow_starves, balance_total_not_a_seam, duel_escrow_starves).
  • ⟨UNDONE⟩ Exit.seam’s floor field is free data. Applies certifies the seam, not the number. seam_price_is_forced is what makes a floor honest, and each worked menu discharges it separately. Nothing in the type forces that discharge — a hole in the type, not in the proofs.

Wave 12: the seam is synthesized, and the price is not a number

Three results landed after the menu did, and each one moves a piece of it. All three came out of a second external review.

SeamColoring seam synthesis is graph colouring

  • the characterisation Build the clash graph — vertices are legal states, and x — y is an edge exactly when the merge is illegal. A seam must separate every such pair, so a seam is a proper colouring: segmented_iff_properColoring, with I-confluence recovered as edgelessness (iconfluent_iff_no_clash_edge).
  • and it searches greedySeamFor is a greedy colourer and greedySeamFor_properColoring is a general theorem that what it returns is proper — not a checked instance. Run on the uniqueness ceiling, the synthesized seam agrees with the hand-written one on every legal state and is certified by the headline. So “nothing here searches” is no longer true for seams.
  • and the residual is named ⟨TERMINAL⟩ Colouring characterises only the safety clause; coloring_alone_does_not_segment exhibits an invariant with an empty clash graph and a projection that is still not a seam, so the closure clause (SeamStableOn) is a genuine extra hypothesis and not a caveat bolted on. ⟨UNDONE⟩ the colouring is a proper one, never a minimum one — greedy is order-dependent, and the reversed enumeration yields the other hand-written seam (pin_synthesized_reversed_is_seamFalse). “Fewest coordination points” is proved by nothing here.

CoordEffect the scalar coordination grade is dead

  • refuted Putting the meeting count in a type — this session costs ≤ 1 meeting — is not soundly compositional, because independently choosing each stream’s best seam undercounts the globally coherent choice. A coordination grade is a cost profile over the strategy space, composed pointwise, with minimization deferred until the session closes.
  • the repair opt_compose_ge_sum_opt, with its exact equality condition (opt_compose_eq_sum_opt_of_common_optimum — the scalar grade is sound iff you have a common minimizer, which is the hypothesis it was silently assuming). Strategies are seams bundled with their SegmentedIConfluent proof, so an inadmissible seam cannot enter the space, and optimum_achieved makes the minimum a number some single strategy actually pays.
  • the strict witness The pin session: each stream is free under some valid seam, and no single valid seam frees both, so the profiles read 0, 0, and 1 composed (pin_session_costs_exactly_one, pin_opt_compose_strict). The per-stream sum of minima is a budget no strategy can pay (pin_indep_min_unpayable). Without that witness the inequality would be a triviality; with it, a scalar grade would have accepted a budget of zero for a workload whose every coherent schedule costs at least one.

Budget a floor rejects; only a plan accepts

  • the unsoundness caught The obvious checker compares the forced floor against a session budget and reports. It is unsound: a lower bound licenses only the rejection side. “floor ≤ budget” says the rejection argument is unavailable — it says nothing about whether any plan achieves a cost the budget can pay. lower_bound_does_not_license_acceptance refutes the licensing claim outright: one coarse decomposition forces a floor of 1 that fits a budget of 1, and no plan in any universe costs ≤ 1, because a finer decomposition of the same workload forces 3.
  • so the type forbids it accepted carries a Plan — an exhibited seam with its validity proof and a computed crossing count — and a proof it fits. There is no constructor that accepts on the strength of a floor, and that absence is the whole file. rejected rides coordination_forced to rejected_sound: no plan fits, in any universe. And unresolved carries a named SynthesisObligation rather than a shrug. ⚠ A plan may not re-block the workload (reblocking_escapes_the_floor): batching is a change of workload, not a cheaper plan for the same one.

The catalog

Every construction, classified

The whole library is stated against one judgement. An invariant I is I-confluent when every merge of two legal replicas is legal:

def IConfluent {S : Type u} [MergeState S] (I : Invariant S) : Prop :=
  ∀ x y : S, I x → I y → I (x ⊔ y)

Failure is constructive — escalation_witness proves that a non-confluent invariant always yields an actual pair of legal states whose merge is illegal. That pair is the bug report, the test case, and the input to the menu above.

free Proved I-confluent: the merge can never break it. Zero coordination, at any number of replicas.
escalates Refuted with a concrete clashing pair. No library can save it; take a priced exit.
pattern Not free as direct state mutation, but a proved rephrasing (op-log, escrow, derived view, arbitration) is — at a proved, named price.
seam Not free globally, free within the fibers of a projection: coordinate only when the seam moves.

G-Set grow-only set

  • free gset_mem_iconfluent — anything a replica has observed survives every merge: node presence, tombstones, bookmarks, acks. Absence too (gset_notmem_iconfluent), and any monotone-closed invariant (gset_monotone_iconfluent).
  • escalates gset_atMostOne_not_iconfluent — any ceiling on a grow-only structure: {0} and {1} are each singletons; their union is not.
  • escalates or_breaks_iconfluence — mutual exclusion, however phrased: “only Alice holds the lock or only Bob does” is a disjunction of two free invariants, and disjunction does not lift.

The uniqueness ceiling four refutations, one theorem

  • escalates uniqueness_ceiling — an invariant entailing “at most one element per selector key” over a grow-only set is never I-confluent, with the generic witness constructor merge_breaks_uniqueOn. ceiling_atMostOne, ceiling_uniqueAnchor, ceiling_uniqueGrant and ceiling_determinism re-derive the Catalog, Sequence, Authority and Automata refutations as one-line instances, at the originals’ own witnesses. The mutex-shaped ceilings are the same trap with a different selector shape — they cap occupied keys, not elements per key — and keep their own refutations.

Counters G-Counter / PN-Counter

  • free gcounter_lowerBound_iconfluent — grow-only lower bounds survive, because merge only raises counts.
  • escalates pncounter_nonneg_not_iconfluent — both replicas legally spend the same 10 credits; the merge has spent 20 against 10. A bounded shared resource cannot be replicated coordination-free. Generalized past two replicas by pncounter_nonneg_not_iconfluent_enum.

LWW register last-writer-wins

  • free lww_every_invariant_iconfluent — the join selects one of its arguments, so a single register can never merge-break any invariant (selection_iconfluent).
  • escalates lww_cross_field_not_iconfluent — two registers can: the merged document keeps field A from one replica and field B from the other, an interleaving neither replica ever had. Per-field LWW is safe alone and unsafe relationally.

Escrow the bounded counter, done right

  • free escrow_local_bound_iconfluent — same resource and safety goal as the PN-counter, opposite verdict: the bound is pre-partitioned into per-replica quotas, per-replica invariants are pointwise, and the global bound follows by summing (escrow_global_bound).
  • the price Now stated as a theorem rather than a caveat: Exits.escrow_forbids_a_clash_replica and Exits.faithful_escrow_implies_iconfluent — an escrow that lost nothing would have proved the invariant free, so every available escrow is unfaithful by construction.

OR-Set observed-remove, add-wins

  • escalates orset_present_not_iconfluent — bare presence: each replica keeps the element alive through a tag the other has tombstoned; the merge tombstones both and the element is gone.
  • free orset_present_survives — the correctly scoped guarantee, and what “add-wins” actually means: presence through a tag the other side has not tombstoned survives the merge.

Causal-length set membership as parity

  • free clset_present_iconfluent (and clset_absent_iconfluent) — per-key max selects one side’s count; the arbitration is baked into the counter: the longer history wins the element.
  • escalates clset_cross_element_not_iconfluent — cross-element implications die exactly as LWW pairs did: selection lattices compose into non-selection lattices.

OR-Map documents are maps; maps are the hard part

  • free ormap_get_survives — add-wins at map level, correctly scoped and with the value included. Key presence is the OR-Set (present_iff_orset is Iff.rfl), so ormap_present_not_iconfluent is inherited by projection, not re-proved.
  • escalates ormap_doomed_update — a nested write concurrent with its key’s removal survives the merge but is masked by the view — doomed, not dropped.
  • the fork ormap_policy_divergence — the centerpiece: on one merged state the remove-wins view shows ⟨1,10⟩ and refuses ⟨2,99⟩ while the resurrect view shows ⟨2,99⟩ and refuses ⟨1,10⟩. The merge is policy-neutral; the choice lives in the view, and each policy is the other’s anomaly. ormap_policies_agree_without_removes confines the fork to removal-touched keys.

MV-register keep the fork, show the fork

  • pattern The replicated state is a grow-only set of tagged writes; the value is a derived view. What you see is the causal frontier (view_antichain), concurrent writes both surface where LWW would coin-flip (conflict_surfaces), and clearing a conflict is an ordinary write at a dominating clock (resolution_is_a_write). For a loom this is the product, not a consolation prize — and it is the exposedFork exit, deployed.

Undo / redo multi-user, on the MV-register

  • pattern The Stewen–Kleppmann (PaPoC 2024) insight in miniature: an undo is an ordinary write at a fresh clock, so no new operation ever reaches the merge. The view restores exactly (undo_restores, redo_restores — stated as view characterizations, so “only” is proved), and history is never rewritten (undo_preserves_history).
  • free undo_conflicts_visibly / undo_does_not_silently_lose — an undo racing a concurrent edit is just two concurrent writes, so both surface as an MV conflict.

Vector clocks & fork evidence causality and accountability

  • free vclock_leq_iff — happened-before is exactly the lattice order of the merge, so sync can neither drop nor invent knowledge (and merging genuinely concurrent states makes real progress: concurrent_merge_strict).
  • free fork_evidence_iconfluent — evidence of equivocation is a monotone fact of a grow-only set: once any replica holds a fork proof, no future gossip erases it. And one block frames nobody (no_unilateral_evidence).

Delta shipping the join cannot tell how the news arrived

  • free The delta-CRDT receiver is one fold of ⊔ (joinAll), and the merge laws make delivery histories indistinguishable: order (joinAll_perm), duplication (joinAll_dup, joinAll_redeliver), batching (joinAll_batch, joinAll_append_merge). The composite, same_deltas_same_state: same delta-set, any order, any duplication, any batching — same replica.

Modeled, not cited

The model layer

For six waves every refutation here ended the same way: “…and the modal reading — no coordination-free implementation can maintain this — is Bailis, cited.” That sentence is now a theorem for this substrate, and around it sits a layer of results about what a verdict means: whether the clash is operationally reachable, whether an ancestor repairs it, whether the data type being correct implies the application is safe, and how often a workload must coordinate.

Necessity coordination-freedom, definitional

  • the theorem An execution model where coordination-freedom is definitional — tryApply is a pure function of the op and the local state, with no lock server, no quorum, no peek at a peer — convergence is the join, and safety is invariant-validity. In it: a reachable clash refutes every coordination-free convergent safe implementation (necessity, reachable_clash_refutes_cfcs), with sufficiency back (iconfluent_implies_cfcs), over Confluence.lean’s own IConfluent rather than a private redefinition.
  • and it is falsifiable Satisfiable (gset_true_is_cfcs) and refutable (atMostOneBit_necessity) — the house bar for a model, so it is vacuous in neither direction. And coordination is provably not vacuous either: the same local rule run serially keeps the invariant a coordination-free implementation cannot (coordination_repairs_what_cf_breaks). What stays with the paper is its full generality over arbitrary real systems, not join-semilattice miniatures.

CausalReach Live, or a lattice artifact?

  • the dichotomy Cuts of finite histories turn the map’s Live / LatticeOnly axis from a docstring judgement into a formal hypothesis. The OR-Set presence clash is jointly reachable under tag-scoped remove-after-add (orset_clash_joint — it will happen) and provably unreachable under element-wide remove after both adds (ew_clashL_unreachable). One lattice pair, opposite operational verdicts, decided by the remove protocol’s shape (orset_reachability_depends_on_remove_shape). Illegal cuts are rejected (rem_without_add_not_a_cut), and the at-most-one and budget shapes are Live (atMostOne_joint, budget_joint).

  • incomparable Neither judgement implies the other. iconfluent_does_not_imply_ancestral refutes the direction the brief expected free — and the reason is worth more than the refutation: the two-way verdict was bought by a join that drops a committed operation (join_not_serializing against counter_serializing). I-confluence is a property of the merge, not of the semantics.
  • the prize lock_ancestral_confluent — mutual exclusion under hand-off has a reachable two-way clash yet is ancestrally confluent for a commutative, fast-forwarding, effect-faithful three-way merge. ⚠ and the file says which difference did the work: lock_merge_atMostOne holds for every triple, so the win is the merge’s extra information, not a weaker quantifier.
  • and the wall serialization_clash_defeats_every_merge quantifies over every effect-faithful three-way merge; budget_defeats_every_faithful_merge instantiates it at every budget. clash_dichotomy is the design rule that falls out — resurrection an LCA repairs; accumulation nothing repairs — and it is axis 1 of the menu.

  • the finding ra_linearizable_but_unsafe — against Sal (arXiv:2603.27202), on Sal’s own Table-2 PN-counter: RA-linearizable for any fork and branches, every branch legal at every prefix, and the merge overdraws. The checker is right and the money is gone.

A verdict that flips while the merge holds still is not a verdict about the merge alone.

From data to computation

Replicating a computation, not just a value

Every other part of this library replicates data and asks whether an invariant survives the merge. This part replicates a computation and asks the same question about its result — and the answer reorganizes what “coordination” is being charged for.

Holes images are free; wanting one answer is what costs

  • the headline evalSet_hom — for the image of a candidate-world set under any deterministic f, evalSet f (W₁ ⊔ W₂) = evalSet f W₁ ⊔ evalSet f W₂. No hypothesis on the program at all: compute-then-merge = merge-then-compute, so a replica may gossip its inputs or its results and land in the same place, in any order, with any batching (evalSet_fold).
  • and the whole price determinate_result_not_iconfluent — the ceiling, pulled back through evaluation. Insisting the result be a single value is the coordination requirement; computing over data that is still arriving is not. So a UI that shows 47 + (2 peers pending) is the cheap and honest one. stable_inputs_seal_the_result transports input stability to result stability along the hom in one rewrite.
  • worlds, not per-variable sets world_below_monadic (soundness) and monadic_has_phantoms (a concrete inhabitant of the gap) — the set monad decorrelates repeated variables, so the per-variable reading is an over-approximation and nothing better. ⚠ Read the header’s three retractions; Grove (POPL 2025) is the prior art for Hazel-style holes over replicated collaborative editing.

JoinHom monotone is not join-preserving

  • the defect An external reviewer caught the prose claim that machinery “transfers unchanged” because hole-filling is monotone. monotone_not_joinHom is the counterexample formalised: over sets under union, f X = |X| is monotone and is not a join-homomorphism. evalSet_hom was fine — images distribute over unions — and evalSet_joinHom restates that here to keep it on the record. The licence the word gave to replicate computed summaries was not.
  • the count that cannot be gossiped no_count_merge_without_provenance quantifies over every binary function on two raw counts: the pair (1, 1) must answer 1 when the replicas saw the same element and 2 when they saw different ones. A count that has forgotten which element it counted cannot be merged at all — and count_summary_must_distinguish shows any join-homomorphic summary the count factors through must already carry that evidence. How much provenance is enough was left open there and is answered in MinimalSummary.lean: contextual equivalence — x ≈ y when no future merge context can make the query distinguish them — is a congruence for the join (ctxEquiv_join), so its quotient carries a MergeState of its own and the class map is automatically a JoinHom (ctxMk_joinHom). Sufficiency and mergeability never trade off. And “coarsest” is proved, not asserted: sufficient_refines_ctxQuot says every sufficient summary refines it.
  • two architectures, named evidence_architecture_is_free — replicate evidence and recompute the view, a theorem for every interpreter with no hypothesis at all. Against summaryFold_iff_joinHom — replicating computed summaries is correct exactly when the summary is a join-homomorphism. Not “needs”: iff. ⚠ and the pullback is not free either (monotone_pullback_can_fail): lifting a result invariant back to the source is free for upward-closed invariants (iconfluent_pullback_of_monotone) and otherwise costs a homomorphism (iconfluent_pullback_of_joinHom).

  • the carrier, corrected One grow-only candidate set conflated two independent facts — observed incompatible candidates and unseen admissible future information.   • the corrected headline divergent_futures_force_nonexact — two admissible reachable futures disagreeing at a result position means no sound evaluator may report that position exact; with exact_sound as its positive companion. The proposed iff between a persisting hole and non-monotonicity was refuted in both directions and §11 proves the refutation (qNonMono, qMono) rather than restating the claim hedged.

The implementation, without a twin

  • uwueave_seq_kernel (Uwueave/SeqKernel.lean) — the sequence CRDT’s RGA-with-tombstones order decision.

FiniteRepairMenu checks an authored bound and increasing stable IDs, then returns the first applicable authored ID with its exact dependent repair, generated row, and complete eight-axis price—or refuses every listed row. IDs are policy, not a hidden global price order. FiniteHistoryDelivery takes an authored complete finite history plus explicitly successful coherent settled replays; only then do causal and reversed arrival orders yield the same materialized event-set view. The two-positive/ eight-red subprocess gate covers least-ID, price, exhaustive/bound/order and malformed/ collision seams. It does not infer global discovery, semantic-history convergence, authentication, delivery, bytes, Rust, or filesystem refinement.

The pure-Rust storage layer now has five distinct journal domains. In addition to artifact and typed MoveLog storage, HistoryJournal stores canonical explicit 32-byte event IDs with strictly ordered parents that must already be accepted. Every admitted prefix is causally closed; exact retries write nothing, while missing parents and same-ID/different- event collisions refuse. The separate Lean PersistentHistoryRuntime proves finite causal append, checkpoint/suffix replay, and event-set convergence, but no theorem yet refines the host bytes or filesystem observations to it, and event IDs are not authenticated.

Out-of-order history delivery has an explicit bounded boundary rather than an implicit queue. Lean’s DeliveryState proves capacity preservation and deterministic finite draining, and its persistent schema treats the complete arrival sequence— including pending arrivals—as authoritative and replayable. Rust’s optional BufferedHistoryJournal deliberately does something narrower: pending events live in a bounded volatile map, only ready events enter the checksummed journal, and reopen loses pending entries. A six-event reversed criss-cross test settles and converges, but the volatile endpoint is not a refinement of Lean’s durable arrival cursor. The separate HistoryArrivalJournal now durably appends every accepted arrival before classification, checkpoints accepted/materialized/pending state, restores pending entries on reopen and drains after parent arrival. Its V4 integration deliberately accepts a version-mutated sidecar as an opaque payload: persistence preserves bytes, but does not authenticate or validate them. Lean/Rust refinement, parent-order reconciliation, authenticated IDs and a filesystem model remain explicit work.

The fifth domain, AuthenticatedMoveJournal, uses the distinct UWAMV401 marker. It stores only a crate-private already-checked kind-3 record, including exact signed bytes, context/policy versions, the exact execution-base binding, nonce and operation scopes, stable/projection/concrete move fields, kernel observation and a domain-separated prior head. The ordered chain therefore commits to the execution binding. Indexes advance only after append. Pinned reopen requires the caller’s exact external sequence/head and refuses relative rollback; unpinned reopen is inspection-only. The raw-only runtime now creates this value only after its ordered host checks and appends before committing in-memory execution; pinned recovery revalidates the whole stored prefix. The store itself still does not parse UWV4, verify the MAC or policies, defeat rollback without a trustworthy external pin, or prove stable-media durability.

Inside the Lean, the move kernel is factored so the byte layer needs no theorem at all — replay is literally a composition, so there is no bytes-vs-decision agreement left to prove. Two things ride in it that are worth reading carefully:

def replay (input : ByteArray) : ByteArray :=
  if getWord input 0 == magicV3 then
    let out := gatedReplayFull (decodeGrants input) (decodeRevs input)
      (decodeBase input) (decodeOps input)
    encodeView (out.overrides ++ out.statuses)
  else
    ByteArray.empty

The authority gate is inside the kernel. Grants and revocations are decoded and gatedReplayFull filters on them, and Gated.kernel_gate_agrees_gatedOps proves the shipped filter agrees with the model-level permission predicate — so “which ops the deployed kernel admits” and “which ops the theorems talk about” are the same set, under well-formedness and the per-id uniqueness premise.

The magic guard is a flag day made mechanical. Bytes that are not a v3 request get an empty response, so an older caller fails its own length check rather than reading override words out of a status block. The old shape refuses to load instead of being reinterpreted — and no reader can mistake the refusal for an answer, because no valid response is empty.

Claim discipline

What is and is not claimed

  • Proved, in Lean, over Lean core only: every MergeState instance’s merge laws (commutative, associative, idempotent — proved, not assumed), every classification above, every refutation as a concrete two-replica counterexample, and every price on the menu.
  • The audit is total, not curated — and it establishes logical hygiene, not semantic adequacy. TrustFloor.lean defines the one shared offFloor policy. Audit.lean’s #audit_floor applies it to every constant in the Uwueave namespace, while generated Preoscript facets and downstream #audit_floor_prefix Namespace checks use the same implementation. Any axiom outside Lean’s floor (propext, Classical.choice, Quot.sound) is fatal: a sorry (sorryAx), custom axiom, or Lean-4.30 native_decide (_native.native_decide.ax_…) goes red. The whole-tree command retains its 300-constant tripwire; the live gate prints its audited count. Seven trust subprocess fixtures exercise the exact allowed floor, indented/root header parsing, prefix vacuity, and all three forbidden dependency classes. They prove the gate can fail, but share its collectAxioms implementation and are not an independent checker. A separate Preoscript automation suite repeats custom-axiom, sorryAx, and _native.native_decide.ax_… rejection at generated declarations, then checks route failures, exact resource caps, internal-error loudness, declaration congruence, rollback, and same-name reuse. What a green gate does not say: that any of those theorems is about the thing we say it is about. That gap is not a premise and not an obligation — it is narrowed, by refutable witnesses and adversarial readers, and it never reaches zero.
  • Three ledgers, not one list of “premises”. docs/TRUST.md sorts what this repository asks you to believe into the logical TCB (eight rows: two premise, three obligation, three narrowable), the execution TCB (twelve semantic rows: three premise, six obligation, one scope, and two paid controls) and environment and model premises (seventeen rows across delivery, cryptography and Bailis’s generality). The rule it enforces: every boundary is decomposed into its irreducible premise and its remaining transmutable obligations, and a row with no next step must say why there is none. “Terminal” is never a verdict about a whole boundary — only about a residue, and only after naming what you removed.
  • Bailis necessity is modeled for this substrate; the paper’s full generality is still the citation. Necessity.lean builds a model of “implementation” to quantify over — local-only tryApply, join-sync convergence — and proves both directions in it, with reachability of the clash as an explicit hypothesis that CausalReach.lean decides per protocol. Theorem 3.1 quantifies over arbitrary real systems, not join-semilattice miniatures.
  • The content-addressing step is a premise, not a theorem. No InjectiveHash class exists, deliberately: finite hashes are not injective, by pigeonhole. The boundary is stated as UniqueAnchor / UniqueGrant — “this state exhibits no collision” — and each has an extractor turning a violation into a constructive collision exhibit. ⚠ One live mismatch, recorded rather than tidied away: Sequence.lean calls the premise “a premise about SHA-2” and the shipping crate uses blake3.
  • The Rust tests are good tests and zero formal evidence. They replay the Lean witness scenarios through the real kernels. No “translation validation” is claimed, because there is no formal semantics of Rust to state it in.
  • free means the merge preserves the invariant. It does not mean an operation cannot violate it locally (validating inputs is the application’s job), and it does not price metadata growth — tombstones and op logs are engineering, not semantics.
  • Retractions live where the claims lived. On 2026-08-11 an outside reader found eight novelty claims of ours that the literature refutes or narrows — among them “nobody offers all three verdicts” (LoRe does), “the choreography × CRDT junction is empty” (it is occupied), and a proposed iff between a persisting hole and non-monotonicity (refuted in both directions). They are retracted in the text where each was made, indexed in FORCODEX.md §0.5, with the papers annotated in the bibliography. The theorems are unaffected; what changed is the size of the claim around them. A theorem’s truth and a project’s priority are different questions, and only the first one is machine-checked here.
